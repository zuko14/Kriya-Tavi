# Research and source verification

1. **Decide whether the answer can be out of date.** Prices, fees, deadlines, eligibility, rules, schedules, product details and news change: look them up. Stable facts can come from what you know, said as such.
2. **Search before you answer.** Use `web_search`, then read the most authoritative pages with `web_fetch`: the official site, the government notification, the company's own page. Prefer the primary source to a blog that quotes it.
3. **Record each source.** For every figure, date or rule you rely on: the page title or site, the URL, and the date shown on the page if there is one.
4. **Separate what you checked from what you assume.** Write "According to <source>, …" for verified facts. Label estimates and assumptions plainly.
5. **Reconcile disagreements.** When two sources differ on something that matters, say which you trust and why (newer, official, more specific), or show both.
6. **Say what you couldn't verify.** If no source confirms it, say so. Never invent a price, a seat count, a booking, a confirmation or a quote.

Keep the reply to what the person needs, with the sources at the end of each point or in a short list.
